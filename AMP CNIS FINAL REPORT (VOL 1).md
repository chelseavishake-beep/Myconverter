# CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

## Background 

The Department of Water Resources and Irrigation (DWRI) is a government organization responsible for planning, developing, maintaining, operating, managing, and monitoring various environmentally sustainable and socially acceptable irrigation projects, both surface and groundwater, especially larger ones. Many of these systems are jointly managed by the DWRI and respective Water Users Associations (WUAs), but their performance has generally been below average. Efforts to enhance these systems performance through Participatory Irrigation Management or Irrigation Management Transfer have been made, but several factors still hinder their effectiveness.

Recognizing these challenges, the DWRI, through the Irrigation Management Division (IMD), has initiated a series of integrated action plans to improve irrigation system management, ensuring sustainable operation and increased efficiency. Recently, the DWRI developed Management, Operation, and Maintenance (MOM) guidelines, currently awaiting approval. These guidelines cover all critical aspects needed for the proper management of irrigation systems. One key component emphasized in the guidelines is the preparation and implementation of an Asset Management Plan (AMP) for the irrigation system.

In irrigation systems, assets include all infrastructure and equipment related to the system, such as canals, associated structures, water sources, canal right-of-ways, vegetation, buildings, and equipment. The guidelines define AMP as a process for sustainable maintenance planning, aimed at providing users with reliable and affordable irrigation services. A comprehensive and well-designed asset management framework offers numerous benefits, including improved irrigation system performance, better returns, enhanced cost efficiency, user satisfaction, and avoidance of performance failures.

Thus, the objective of the AMP is to outline a roadmap for the sustainable management and maintenance of irrigation canals and associated structures for optimal use by irrigation office and the local community. The AMP should include an asset survey to identify their physical and functional status, maintenance planning, implementation, monitoring, and quality control mechanisms. This study aims to clearly define all the significant aspects needed to prepare the Asset Management Plan for CNIS.

## Rationale of the assignment

As a primarily agrarian nation, Nepal's economy heavily relies on agriculture, which constitutes over 35% of its GDP and employs more than 74% of its workforce. Consequently, the agriculture sector is prioritized by the nation, receiving significant resource allocation in each periodic plan. The vitality of the irrigation system directly affects agricultural productivity, which in turn drives economic growth.

The agricultural sector, contributing more than one-third of Nepal's GDP and serving as the primary source of employment, is essential for the livelihoods of its citizens. The expansion and efficient management of agriculture are imperative for increasing income levels, reducing poverty, and elevating living standards. However, the sector's performance has not kept pace with the rising food demand and livelihood requirements of the expanding population. Against the backdrop of recent global and national food crises and unprecedented spikes in food prices, the role of agriculture in mitigating food shortages remains crucial. Least developed countries, reliant on agriculture, aim to alleviate poverty and address economic challenges.

The Asset Management Plan (AMP) serves as a valuable tool in maximizing the performance and lifespan of irrigation assets by outlining strategies for regular maintenance, repairs, and upgrades. This optimization leads to improved water delivery efficiency, reduced downtime, and enhanced reliability of the irrigation system. An AMP enables the identification and mitigation of risks associated with asset defects of Irrigation system. By systematically assessing asset condition and performance, potential risks such as water distribution, infrastructure failures, or environmental impacts can be anticipated and addressed, reducing the likelihood of costly disruptions or accidents.

To address these challenges, the Department of Water Resources and Irrigation (DWRI) has initiated recommendations for the management, operation, and maintenance of irrigation systems to enhance overall performance. Recognizing that past management, operation, and maintenance actions were often ad hoc, DWRI aims to establish clear guidelines and a standardized strategy for creating MOM plans for each irrigation system. To expediently assess the system's status, DWRI has launched an asset management plan of the Chandra Nahar Irrigation System (CNIS).

## Objective and Scope of the Work

The objective of this report is to prepare an Asset Management Plan (AMP) including the operation and maintenance plan of CNIS and the financial management plan of the Irrigation Office and WUA by conducting required surveys at the field level and by collecting available baseline data from concerned office and the Irrigation Management Division, DWRI, Jawalakhel.

In order to achieve the above objectives, the scope of the work for this assignment is, but not limited to:

- Collection and review of existing documents and literature regarding the assets, their physical conditions, operational status and needs to rehabilitate for their functionality;

## Methodology

The major objective of this task is the preparation of Asset management plan of Chandra Nahar Irrigation Project. Additionally, it emphasizes on the delivery of operation and maintenance plan of CNIS and its financial management plan. The Department of Water Resources and Irrigation (DWRI) has guidelines on Management, Operation and Maintenance (MOM) of Irrigation Systems, which include a comprehensive guideline for the preparation of the AMP. Asset Management Plan (AMP) for the Chandra Nahar irrigation system involves several key steps to ensure effective management and maintenance of assets. The following methodology outlines the process:

1.  **Desk Study**

The initial phase involves conducting thorough desk research on the Chandra Nahar irrigation system. Relevant materials from the Chandra Nahar Irrigation Project, gathered from DWRI, will be collected, reviewed, and analyzed. Additionally, journals, articles, and web resources related to the project will be accessed for comprehensive understanding. This cost-effective and efficient approach provides essential foundational knowledge for the project.

2.  **Data collection**

This is the second step of our task, which simply is the collection of all available resources, materials and information from various official sources that can be useful to our assigned task. These data can be in the form of maps, previous reports on the Chandra Nahar irrigation project, Asset inventory Report on CNIS, MOM guidelines, newspaper articles, maps, drawings and other aerial photographs. To become familiar with the study area, there is no substitute of data collection for deskwork and study. The various sources shall be used for the collection of secondary data some of which are**:**

- Previous progress reports and other reports on the Chandra Nahar irrigation project prepared by DWRI and other agencies.

- Annual audit reports

- Data related to irrigation service fee collection and the rates of each distributary.

- Information on WUA of Chandra Nahar Irrigation from Koshi pump Chandra Nahar Irrigation management office.

- Previous Asset Inventory reports and MoM guidelines

- Topo maps, District administrative map of District, Arial photographs and District Irrigation Map if available

- Digital GIS data Excel sheets relevant to irrigation network or irrigation area.

- Financial and technical Data regarding Chandra Nahar IS

- Other relevant reports

3.  **Asset Inventory and Condition Assessment**

In this step, a thorough inventory of all components encompassing the Chandra Nahar Irrigation infrastructure, encompassing canals, gates, structures, and control systems is done. The essential data regarding structure’s location, physical condition and performance is reviewed from the previous reports. However, as the condition might have deteriorated further or possibly improved (field condition may have changed, where previous report’s result is insufficient; the field work/survey/investigations are carried out. The condition of each asset in terms of functionality and structural stability is accessed in its present state.

4.  **Risk Assessment and Criticality Analysis**

After all field data are collected and verified, these structural data are subjected to potential risks and vulnerabilities associated with them, considering factors like failure likelihood, consequence of failure, and impact on system performance. The following formula can be used to estimate the level of risk.

*Risk = Probability or chance that something happens x Effect or Impact when that happens*

5.  **Risk Mitigation and cost analysis**

Once the risk associated with each asset is identified, the mitigation action to prevent or reduce these risks is identified. The roles are assigned to different authorities based on the technical and financial aspects of the mitigation work.

6.  **Financial planning**

Based on the cash flow analysis of the CNIS, its annual income and expenditure are estimated. Expenditure sheets associated with operation, maintenance and management are prepared separately whereas the income sheets from membership fees, donations and ISF are prepared. The Adjustment of Rates to be executed on the ISF per ha will be identified for counter balancing the O & M cost.

7.  **Development of Operation and Maintenance Strategies**

The smooth operation of an irrigation system is identified by the justifiable and dispute free distribution of irrigation water in the field (from Head to tail section). Thus, this shall be ensured by making operation strategies by optimization of various factors such as discharge rates, gate operation and irrigation frequencies.

The maintenance strategies for each asset category (Bridge type, Flow regulating type) will be prepared and the responsible authority (Farmers, WUA, Irrigation Office etc.) will be assigned considering factors like asset criticality, condition, and budget constraints. Furthermore, appropriate maintenance activities, frequencies, and methodologies, including preventive, routine, and Periodic maintenance approaches.

While operating the system, the application of the exact amount of water required by the crop at the right time is the main achievement and hence the farmers or operators need to know elements such as water discharge and rate, operating hours and irrigation frequency. Properly operated, maintained irrigation system and networks enable farmers to exercise absolute control over water use at farm level. Thus, it is easy for them to apply irrigation schedules based on crop, soil, weather, and water availability and quality factors.

There are two types of maintenance work for asset management i.e. preventive: done to keep the structures and canals in the desired condition, and Corrective: done after damage has occurred and it restores the system to the desired performance level. The well-known maintenance are as follows:

- **Regular or Routine maintenance**: It is preventive maintenance includes all small repairs work like greasing of gates, maintaining canal banks, small desilting, weed removing etc. on a regular basis throughout the year to keep the irrigation system functioning.

## Historical background

The Chandra Canal, the first government-built irrigation system in Nepal, is situated on the right bank of the Triyuga River, which is a tributary of the Koshi River in Saptari District, was built in 1979 B.S. (1923 AD TO 1927 AD). The Government of India (GOI) provided funding for the project’s initial renovation. Subsequently, a pump canal, a distribution system, and another project for rehabilitating the Chandra Canal was implemented. The Chandra Canal Irrigation Project had an annual budget of only NRs. 1, 25,000 making it challenging to understand how such a substantial irrigation system could be operated within such constraints. Finally, the Indian government agreed to provide grant support for the aforementioned projects, including Chandra Canal rehabilitation. As a result, an Irrigation system was constructed, developed, and renovated to irrigate agricultural land in Saptari district, Nepal.

## Study area

The Chandra Nahar Irrigation System (CNIS), Nepal's first planned irrigation system, was constructed nearly a century ago in the Saptari district of eastern Terai to provide water for agriculture. It marked the beginning of modern irrigation development in Nepal and has played a crucial role in the country's irrigation development since the introduction of periodic planning in 2013 B.S. The CNIS features a 28.6 km-long Chandra main canal with 12 branch canals, irrigating 12097 hectares of land. Due to its extensive canal network and large command areas, managing CNIS became a challenge. To address this, the government introduced a policy for joint management with Water User's Associations (WUAs). As a result, CNIS is now jointly managed by the Department of Water Resources and Irrigation (DWRI) and the WUAs.

## Headwork

The head work of Chandra Canal Irrigation Project, located in Fattepur, Saptari District, is operating efficiently and remains in a stable condition. The intake structure contains 10 bays with a dimension of 3.5m for each bay. All gates are properly functioning, although they require cleaning, oiling, and greasing to ensure optimal performance.

Within the headwork of the CNIS, there is under-sluice structure with a partition, which is functioning well, except for the launching apron slab, which is significantly damaged and requires rehabilitation. The fish ladder, structurally, is in pleasing condition, but proper cleaning is required to maintain its functionality.

## Canal network

The canal network of CNIS comprises of a 28.6 Km long main canal, 12 branch canals (distributaries), 2 branch canals and 2 number of minor canals. The Main canal is an earthen gravity flow contour canal, which begins at the headwork site in Fattepur and ends near the Khando River at Rajbiraj. It has a design capacity of 11.71 cumecs, which is the available water of the river during monsoon. The bed width of the main canal varies from 12m to 6m and the right bank of this canal is 2m wide whereas the left bank has 4m width. The Left bank serves as a service road for inspection and travel purposes for the office and villagers.

The 12 branch canal systems are characterized by having different canal capacities and command areas. These branch canals vary in their characteristics and infrastructure. Some of the branch canals have cross regulators, while some do not have. All the branch canals have head regulator to control the flow of water. Out of 12 branching canals, only six positions have cross regulators installed.

## WUA structure

The WUA coordination committee of CNIS was established and officially registered in the year 2055 BS under the Water Resources Act (1993). This committee is composed of a President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer, and 29 other members. Situated in Kanchanrup municipality, Kanchanpur, near the distributary, the WUA office actively engages in project planning, implementation, and ensuring the efficient operation of the irrigation system.

The annual general assembly, with 257 general members, is convened each year, and elections are conducted every four years. The same president is in charge of office since 2055 B.S. Adhering to WUA by-laws, the organization has ensured 33 percent female representation, with six female members actively participating in the committee. Remarkably, no gender discrimination in wage parity has been observed.

The WUA's primary responsibility is the collection of Irrigation Service Fee (ISF) from beneficiaries per cropping session, charging a nominal fee of 2 Rupees per kattha. The annual collection from the ISF for this irrigation system is estimated to be around one-lakh rupees.

## Risk Assessment and Criticality Assessment

After asset inventory with criticality assessment was performed, a list of possible failures of each component with different reasons/causes why asset fails are listed. More important than the cause, the impact on the structural stability and water delivery will be, hence it is therefore important that one assess the risk of each failure separately. The following formula was used to estimate the level of risk.

The effect score was calculated by taking the average value of the Impact scores of each structure based on their Structural ability and water delivery scores. The **Table 4-10** shows a column with four types of effects. If any structure goes defective but has hardly any effect on the Structural stability or water delivery, it will be given a score of one (non-critical). If this defect would result in a complete loss of functionality of the system in terms of water delivery or structural stability, it gets a score of four (critical).

The probability of failure was given for each structure from high to minimal with the respective scores from four to one. In the row below the table, the probability is assessed. A probability score of one is given if the risk of that part breaking down is very low, or minimal. If the part is very likely to break down a probability score of four is given. Scores 2 and 3 are in between. The final risk score is then calculated by taking the product of these two scores. Thus, the criticality assessment of each component to fail was estimated which is called risk assessment using the following formula.

***Risk = Probability or chance that something happens x Effect or Impact when that happens***

# CHAPTER 5: MAINTENANCE PLANNING

Maintenance planning of any irrigation system is a critical process that ensures the long-term functionality and efficiency of the infrastructure. A systematic approach is to be developed in CNIS for identifying, scheduling, and implementing maintenance activities that keep the system in optimal condition. This includes regular inspections to assess the physical and operational status of Main canals, distributaries, pumps, regulating gates, and other structures, as well as timely repairs and replacements of worn or damaged parts. Effective maintenance planning also incorporates preventive measures to address potential issues before they escalate into major problems, thereby reducing downtime and avoiding costly emergency repairs. By integrating comprehensive asset management practices, including the use of advanced technologies, maintenance planning not only enhances the reliability and performance of Chandra nahar irrigation system but also maximizes the return on investment and ensures the sustainability of water resources for agricultural use.

## Irrigation System Maintenance

Effective irrigation system maintenance aims to ensure the optimal functioning and longevity of irrigation infrastructure while maximizing water efficiency and minimizing environmental impact. Key objectives include regular inspection of components such as pipes, Regulating Gates, and Outlets to identify leaks, clogs, weed and vegetation growth or damage that could impede water flow or distribution. Cleaning and clearing of debris from outlets and gates are essential to maintain system efficiency. Calibration of equipment such as control gates ensures accurate scheduling and water application, preventing over- or under-irrigation. Additionally, monitoring soil moisture levels helps adjust irrigation schedules based on crop water requirement and environmental conditions, promoting water conservation and crop yield.

Overall, comprehensive maintenance efforts of an Irrigation system aim to optimize water use efficiency, promote sustainable resource management, and preserve the integrity of the irrigation infrastructure.

## Existing System and Status of Collection

Since there is only one committee, it is responsible to collect the service fee and distribute as 80% to the CNIS office and 20% for their maintenance purposes. Proper records of the service fee collections are not available now, however the collected data indicated that certain portion of the service fee are collected and used for their regular maintenance. 5 years ago, in the fiscal year 2075/76 an amount rounding up to one lakh was collected. Same amount was collected in the year 2079/80, which suggests an average of one lakh is collected per years as ISF. The rate of the service fee is Nrs 2 per kattha per crop (Nrs 40 per Bigha, 20 Kattha = 1Bigha), which is quite insufficient to maintain the systems. It was also reported that the farmers contribute voluntary labor contribution for cleaning the canal in some branch canal, this works has not been counted in the service fee. At the initial period of the running of the canal, the farmers were enthusiastic to pay the service fee, but the collection works are not active from a decade.

Implementation Monitoring focuses on tracking the progress of asset management activities and ensuring that they are executed as planned.
